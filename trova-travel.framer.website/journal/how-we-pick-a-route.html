How we choose a route

Every route on this site was walked, questioned, and walked again before it ever got a name.

4 min read

May 12, 2026

We walk it before we trust it

Every route on this site was walked by a guide before anyone else knew it existed. A path can look complete on a map and fall apart the moment you're standing on it — a landslide that reroutes the ground every spring, a viewpoint that's stunning in photos and crowded with other groups by nine in the morning. None of that shows up until someone's actually there to see it.

So before a route gets a name or a listing, one of us has walked it front to back, more than once, across more than one season. We're not just checking that it's beautiful. We're checking that it holds up.

Most of what we scout doesn't make it

We've walked coastlines that were incredible for the first hour and repetitive for the next four. We've found summits with views so good they didn't need a group of eight — better suited to two people and a quiet morning than a scheduled trip. Saying no to a route is as much a part of this work as saying yes to one.

What makes it through has earned a place on a short list, not a long catalog. We'd rather know six routes completely than sixty once — that's the whole reason our offering stays small instead of trying to cover more ground than we can actually vouch for.

What changes once a route is chosen

Getting picked isn't the end of the process. A route stays on rotation only if it keeps holding up — season after season, group after group. If conditions shift enough that we can't stand behind it anymore, it comes off the list, no matter how well it photographed the first time we walked it.

That's the difference between a route we found and a route we chose. One is a guess. The other is something a guide already knows — where the ground gets loose, where the light is best in the morning, where to stop and where to keep moving.
